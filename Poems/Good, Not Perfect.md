# Good, Not Perfect

I wanted it. Forty-four years I wanted it.
The perfect grades. The perfect trajectory.
The perfect rescue. The perfect crime, and I mean that,
I built it like a franchise and it was *beautiful*
right up until the wire. And then, of course,
the perfect sobriety. Each day flawless.
Each mark on the calendar an A-plus
in the introductory course of Being Alive.

I wanted the universe to run on merit.
On fairness. On the syllabus. I did the reading.
And the universe handed back a dead sister,
a sabotaged dream, two wives in the ground,
a cell, a gutted condo, a charge I didn't earn,
an insurance company with a form letter,
and a system that called itself civilization
and ran like a franchise for extracting hope
from whoever needed it most.

So I read Genesis. In a cell. Between Clancy and Patterson.
Not for God. For something to do with my eyes.
And I counted. The Maker looks at the light: *good.*
The sky: *good.* The water, the land, the crawling things:
*good, good, good.* Seven times. Never once *perfect.*
Never once *exactly as I drew it up.*
Just: made. Mine. Enough for today. Next.

You understand what that did to me?
The Creator of the universe looked at the whole
messy sprawling unfinished project
and gave it a B and went to bed.

Now I have a bucket under a drip.
The drip is fixed. I paid a plumber. The bucket stays,
because fixes are provisional and buckets are insurance
and a man who's been fixed before knows the difference.
The counter is plywood. The couch has a dip
from a man and four dogs and however many nights.
The dip is comfortable. The dip is ours.
The photograph is from Walgreens. It's fine. It's good.

I'm not enough. I'm not trying to be enough.
I'm present. Accounted for. Still on the roster.
Still answering when the name gets called.
Life is not perfect. It never signed for that.
But as a sister taught me, and a dog keeps teaching me,
it does, at minimum, need to be good.

Good. Lowercase. Seven times.
Then go feed the dogs.
