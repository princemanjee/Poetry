# Load-Bearing

In our house, loyalty was not a virtue.
It was the plumbing. You did not thank it.
You did not discuss it. You did not leave,
because leaving was a verb our family
had never bothered to conjugate.
My father built that. My father, who left.
The rules outlast the man. The building outlasts the architect.
I walked those halls every day of my life.

So when I raised them, I raised them in that building.
Fed them the plumbing. Taught them the walls.
Said: this is what a brother does. He shows up.
He shows up before he's asked, because being asked
is not the trigger. The need is the trigger.
You see the ribs, you stop the truck.
That's the whole religion. I didn't make it up.
I just made sure they had it memorized.

They memorized it.
They can recite it, I'm sure, at dinner,
to their own kids, with feeling.
*Your uncle taught us that.* I bet they say that.
I bet it lands.

Because here's what I got wrong about walls.
I thought if I was the load-bearing one,
the others would learn to bear load.
That's not how buildings work. That's not how anything works.
If one wall carries everything,
the other walls learn exactly one thing,
which is that they don't have to.

I taught them I would hold it.
I taught it so well
that when I finally couldn't,
they had no idea what the sound was.
They thought it was weather.
They checked the driveway and went back inside.

I'm not asking for the money. Let me say that plainly.
I never once, not one time, asked for it back.
I'm not asking for the years, or the school I left,
or the somebody I became so they wouldn't have to.
I'm asking for one thing and it's so small
it's almost funny, and here it is:
I wanted them to stand in my doorway
the way I stood in theirs when they were nine and eight
and say the hard thing to my face.
That's it. That's the invoice. A doorway. A face.

Instead I got the architecture of family,
which is a phrase I used to say with pride,
and which I now understand
is what people call the building
after the wall that held it up
has been quietly removed from the plans
and everyone agrees it was always this way.

I'm still standing. I want that noted.
Not because they're holding me up.
Because I'm a wall. It's the only thing I know how to be.
A wall does not get to choose its building.
A wall does not get to ask the roof
why the roof stopped calling.
A wall just stands there, in the wind,
in the middle of a house that used to be full,
carrying whatever's left
and pretending the weight is a choice.

It's not a choice.
It's load.
I was built for it.
They were built by it.
There's the difference.
There's the whole damn difference.
