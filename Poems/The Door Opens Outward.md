# The Door Opens Outward

I noticed that. The door of the place
swings out, away from the building, like the architecture
itself is telling you: go on. Get out. We're done.
I went.

And the air. God, the air. Same nitrogen.
Same oxygen. Same parts per million
of whatever the West Side was cooking that morning.
But this air *moved.* It had options. It could go left.
It could rise. It was not pushed through vents
that smelled like bleach and someone else's despair.
First unmonitored breath in half a year.
It was good. Lowercase. Just good.

A plastic bag. Inventory: one wallet, expired.
Forty-three dollars. One phone, dead.
One photograph, face down, which I did not turn over,
because a man can only carry so much daylight at a time.

I took the bus.
I'd owned limousines at ten. A Navigator. Military transport.
The back of a federal vehicle after they caught me.
Now the CTA, exact change from a Ziploc,
window seat, diesel and humanity,
and I didn't mind either, because neither was floor wax.

The elevator took its time. So did I.
That was new. Nowhere to be.
For thirty years there had been somewhere. Always somewhere.
A dome, a desert, a studio, a courtroom, a cell.
Now: an elevator. A hallway. A door. A key I hoped still worked.

It worked.

And then the sound. Before the sight, the sound.
Four sets of nails on hardwood. Ninety pounds of joy
at a velocity that shepherds should not achieve indoors.
I went down on one knee, because you can't take that standing,
not after six months on a metal bed.
A slam. A lean. A sentry taking my left leg.
And fifteen pounds of white fur planting herself
on my feet. Not beside them. *On* them. Claimed.

I sat on the floor. They arranged themselves
like they'd rehearsed it. Maybe they had.
The place smelled like dog. The floor was where
everyone needed to be. I stayed a long time.

The door opens outward.
I've been through a lot of doors.
That's the only one I ever noticed.
