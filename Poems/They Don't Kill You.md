# They Don't Kill You

They tell you drugs will take your life.
You picture a grave. A young one. A photo
where you look better than you ever did.
That's not the deal. I wish it were that clean.

They don't kill you. That's the whole grift.
They leave you standing, breathing, in the room,
and then they take the room.

They take the life you were building. Board by board.
They take the friends who stood by you, and the ones who feared you,
and the ones who admired you, and the ones who, God help them,
bragged about you to the old ladies at the mosque.
They take the clearance. They take the resume
that looked like three men's careers shuffled and stapled
and made HR reach for the phone and put it down
and reach for it and put it down and pass.

They take your name at the bank.
They take your name at the door.
They take the seven-foot pole nobody will touch you with
and hand you the pole and say *here, hold this, it's yours now.*

And they do it politely. That's the part.
They're not a robber. They're a slow eviction.
Nothing gets kicked in. Nothing bleeds.
You just look up one Tuesday and the couch is gone
and the wife is gone and the brothers don't call
and your mother still loves you, because mothers do,
which is the only thing left in the apartment
and it is not, it turns out, a load-bearing wall.

What did they give me? I did the inventory.
I got laid. Sure. A few times. Fine.
They did not give me back the phone call.
They did not give me back the laugh.
They did not give me a single yard of that last mile.
They took more than everything else combined
and they did it while I was thanking them.

They don't kill you.
I'd almost respect them if they did.
They just take the life
and let you keep the body
and call it a favor.
