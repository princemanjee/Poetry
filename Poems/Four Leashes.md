# Four Leashes

Six-thirty. A bark. Not a request. A notification.
Six-thirty-two: feet on floor.
Six-thirty-five: coffee, black, since the military,
where additions were inefficiency and so was feeling.
Six-forty: four bowls, four styles.
Deliberate. Frantic. Efficient. Eccentric.
Seven o'clock: the leashes.

Four dogs. One elevator. One lobby. One sidewalk.
The tamale guy, who was there before I went away
and will be there after I'm gone, because tamale guys
run on a clock that does not consult your narrative.

And the wind. Let's discuss the wind.
Chicago wind is not weather. It's a municipal employee
who's held the job a thousand years and will not
be persuaded by your jacket to take the day off.
You lean into it. The dogs lean into it.
Everybody on the sidewalk is leaning into something.

One walks at heel like a West Pointer.
One pulls, always, and is losing, and does not mind.
One takes my left leg as her assignment, permanent.
And one, the small one, the acronym,
has found a wrapper near a hydrant that requires
her full fifteen pounds of immediate investigation.

Four leashes. Four directions. Me in the middle.
This is not a metaphor. I want that on the record.
This is a man and some dogs and a fire hydrant.
And also, fine, it's a metaphor.

Because here's what nobody tells you about standing:
it isn't stillness. It's four things pulling
and you not going with any of them.
It's holding what you've got at the speed of patience
while the wind does its job and the L does its job
and the day does whatever the day has planned.

Some mornings the bed is the only thing that makes sense
and the dogs are the only reason it doesn't win.
But the dogs are a good reason.
The dogs are a very good reason.

I named the small one Hope. All caps. With periods.
Hold On, Pain Ends. A prayer disguised as an acronym
disguised as a dog, because some things are too heavy
to say to a person, and a dog will carry anything
you hand her and never once ask why.

She's on my feet again.
Claimed.
