# Pilot Light

My mother said a pessimist is just an optimist
who got hit enough times to learn to flinch.
That's me. That's exactly me. I'd like it on a shirt.
An optimist who kept getting punched in the plan
until the optimism ducked on its own.

But it's still in there.
That's the part they don't put on the shirt.

You know a pilot light? Down in the stove,
that little blue nothing that nobody looks at,
burning at three in the morning with no pot on,
burning through the funeral and the eviction
and the six months and the drip and the frame,
not because anyone asked it to, but because
that is what a pilot light does. It stays lit.
It waits for someone to turn the knob.

I asked my lawyer if he was optimistic.
He said: *I am experiencing the absence of pessimism,
which in this practice is as close as I get.*
I'll take it. I'll take the absence of pessimism.
I'll take step one again. Step one again.
I'll take *cautiously not pessimistic* and frame it.

Because here's the arithmetic of the whole thing:
the count is not a milestone. It's not round.
It's just the next one. And the *nextness* is the point.
Recovery is not a place you drive to and park.
It's a pen mark, then a pen mark, then a pen mark,
and the mark is always the same mark,
and the sameness is the whole entire miracle.

Has he changed? The man. Has he changed?
I honestly don't know. The engine's still running.
Same engine. Installed the day the sky got taken away.
It doesn't care about direction. It only wants to go.
But the driver. Maybe the driver.
Maybe the guy who chooses whether the going
is running or standing.
Or maybe that's a story I tell because
the alternative is a story I can't afford.

I don't know.
And the not knowing is the newest thing I own.

So: tomorrow. The pen. The mark. The coffee. The dogs.
The wind through the walls. The bucket. The lake.
The city that processes you and does not slow down.
The fight, which looks like a life, because it is one.

The flame's still on.
Nobody's cooking.
Somebody could.
