# The Handle

I never left a handle on anything.
Ask anyone. Every decision I ever delivered
came kindly and completely and sanded smooth,
no grip, no edge, nothing for the other person
to get a hand around and pull.
That was the craft. That was the whole craft.

Except with you.
With you I left a handle on. On purpose.
I cut a notch in the one thing I'd never notched
and I said: here. This is where you hold me.
I pledged the life. I pledged the hope, which is worse,
because a life can be paid back in installments
and hope is a lump sum, cash, no receipt.

I put the Norman Rockwell on the table.
The kitchen. The porch. The two kids and the dog
who is not a rescue, for once, just a dog.
I said *this is where we're going* and I meant it
the way I have meant maybe four things in my life,
and I built the road, because I build roads,
and I paved it in your direction.

You read the room. You always could.
Kindness that points toward you, my old friend said,
is sometimes real and sometimes a distraction,
and the difference is in the timing and the audience.
You had the timing. You had the audience.
And I, who swept every doorway I ever walked through,
who clocked the exits and the leverage and the lie,
walked into you with my eyes on the horizon
and never once looked at the floor.

That's the joke. That's the good one.
A man who could read a general's face at forty yards,
who could smell a bribe before the envelope came out,
who learned to spot a wire by how a man holds his beer,
got taken by the one person he stopped scanning.
I stopped scanning because that's what love is.
You told me so. It's in the brochure.

What you took wasn't the money. There's always money.
Not the years. I've lost years before. I've got a system.
You took the version of me I was going to be
standing next to you. He was a good guy.
I'd met him a few times. I was looking forward to him.
He's gone now. He didn't leave a note.
He just isn't coming, and I keep setting a place.

Horses can't be lied to. I've said it my whole life.
They feel what's true in the body and reject the rest.
Turns out I'm not a horse. Turns out a man
will take a hand on the reins that isn't honest
and call it partnership for years
if the hand is warm enough
and he's tired enough
and the trail is straight enough
that he stops paying attention.

Here's the part I can't fix, so I'll just say it.
I don't hate you. That's the betrayal in the betrayal.
Hate would be a handle. I'd have something to hold.
Instead I've got a smooth, cold, finished thing
with no grip on it anywhere,
which is, I'm told, my specialty.

You learned it from me.
You did it better.
