# The Twenty-Mile Rule

I had a rule. I want you to appreciate
the elegance of it. Twenty miles. At all times.
Twenty miles from the product, from the cash,
from the warehouse and the hands that touched the thing.
I managed from a distance. I was good at it.
Distance was the one thing I could build
faster than a lemonade stand at eight years old.

Twenty miles is, in Chicago, forty minutes.
In Indiana it's one bad decision and a cornfield.
On the map it's a circle. In the body it's nothing.
The body doesn't do miles. The body does *yes.*

Here's the joke, and it's a good one, hold on:
I built a ring around the poison
and stood in the middle of it
and called the middle *clean.*
Twenty miles from the drugs.
Zero miles from the reason.

You can outsource the handling.
You can delegate the touching.
You can be so far from the thing
that no jury could ever place you in the room.
And then you can go home and be in the room,
because the room, it turns out, was never in Indiana.
The room was in the chest.
The room has always been in the chest.

There is no twenty-mile rule for wanting.
There is no distance that outruns a kitchen at 2 a.m.
with the lights off and a spreadsheet and a dead wife
and a set of skills that could solve the problem
and a problem that could not be solved by skill.

So now the rule is different. Here's the new rule:
Zero miles. Stand in it. Feel the wind come through the walls.
Don't manage it. Don't franchise it. Don't delegate the feeling
to a horse, or a dog, or a family out of Aleppo.
Stand in the middle of the circle you drew
and admit the circle was a target.

Twenty miles was never a safe distance.
It was just enough room to keep lying.
